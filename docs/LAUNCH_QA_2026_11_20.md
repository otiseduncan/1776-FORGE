# Baldwin Minute Watch launch QA — November 20, 2026

Live site: https://baldwinminutewatch.org/

Application fixes deployed to Worker `1776-forge`, production version `d9e575bd-1768-4b97-9eda-9060d0a5e357`. Application source commit: `b18f9e9`. Staging candidate: `38d6d0aa-67dc-41ab-b763-8257cc965cf5`.

## Findings and repairs

- Meeting Watch previously queried an empty structured-meetings table and displayed no meetings. It now presents the existing source calendar: 296 entries, with agendas, packets, minutes, cancellation notices and recorded availability notes. The source calendar collection runs through November 4, 2026; this is not a claim of coverage through launch day.
- The main search previously omitted the separate preserved archive. It now queries both collections, including 4,888 archive cards across 15 archived jurisdictions. These are catalogue cards, not 4,888 newly reviewed files. Original catalogue aliases and privacy/publication restrictions remain upstream.
- The owner's supplied public video was matched to YouTube title/channel metadata: The Minute Watch Breakdown — Episode 001, video ID 35QnvDaf61U. It has a Meeting Watch embed, direct YouTube link and searchable listing. It is labelled newsroom reporting, separate from official source records. The title match does not certify every statement in the video.
- All 17 existing embedded-minutes references were matched by SHA-256 to nine preserved packets. Meeting Watch links now retain the exact cited PDF page within the on-site archived original.
- Archive type and year filters now work together with search, jurisdiction and agency selection. The records library points visitors to searches across both collections. Record searches accept words occurring in different metadata fields and include dates and original filenames.
- Governing-bodies view now exposes the archived agency directory when the structured officials table is empty. Budget/audit view now links the already-collected budget and financial-report documents rather than implying that no source records exist.
- The homepage removes repeated count tiles, empty map-preview promotion and repeated legal/resource/jurisdiction blocks; those destinations remain in navigation. Its document highlights show source minutes, agendas, packets and reports rather than upload receipts, JSON checks and package index files.
- Visual inspection found and fixed a low-contrast Forge heading and a cramped phone search box. Meeting navigation tabs wrap on narrow screens.

## Remaining content and verification limits

- The local November 13 automated transcript and November 17 combined transcript/audio package have not been imported into the online catalogue. The November 17 text is explicitly an automated draft, not certified minutes; speaker labels and transcript wording need review. Local creation does not prove publication. The November 13 published meeting report remains available separately.
- There are 21 unpublished article drafts in the database, including the placeholder titled Missing Board of Commissioners meeting minutes. These are distinct from the newly linked public Episode 001. Their presence is not evidence that complete reviewed stories or videos were previously published.
- Online archive search covers titles, filenames, agencies, dates, source links and selected screened text. The complete local OCR/transcript index is not online. No universal full-document text search is claimed.
- The original map and historical community collection remain separate preserved views. They are not fully merged into the new map/discussion search indexes. Existing navigation links remain available.
- Archived source notes such as Not yet due describe the collection snapshot, not a current legal conclusion. The interface now states that timing explicitly. No meeting votes or approval status were inferred from packet presence.
- This pass did not certify all historical factual claims, continuously review every video, exhaustively check every external source URL, or re-hash all stored originals. A sampled downloaded packet matched its recorded hash. Some original-source YouTube links could not be confirmed using oEmbed; those responses alone do not establish that the videos are broken.
